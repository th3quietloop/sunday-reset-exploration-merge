# V11: To-dos and the week's plan

Russell, Oct 14, on This week's plan: "I really like this page... The only part on here is the to-dos. I feel like I can check off the to-do, but I don't know how to add or remove. It looks cool, but it doesn't seem very functional. I don't even know how I can get there for the first time."

And on the To-dos panel beside the week: "I really like this because it's kind of easy to see. I don't know if it's easier for us to zoom in on the right-hand side and grab a block, and then the calendar zooms out so we can see it side by side... everything looks very uniform, and it kind of blends in. How can we organize this in a better way, or, if we're going to show the thing on the board, how can it add value?"

Below: what was there, what was wrong from the person's side, and what V11 does now, each marked built or not built.

## One window for what's due

Four places counted "due" four ways: the To-dos button and the stack strip said 6 (the next 7 days), the panel's Due soon card said 7 and "7 in three weeks" (21 days), the plan said "4 more, due this week, need a time" (the calendar week, and it counted the half timed report twice), and the reset said "6 due this week, 4 without a time" and "7 due soon have no time yet".

- **Built.** One window: due this week means due in the next 7 days, overdue included, the same to-dos the board's own To do count and the notepad have always counted. It's said "due this week" everywhere: the button, the strip, the bench, the panel, the plan, the reset's first page, and its To-dos step.
- **Built.** Counts never run wider than 99+.
- **Built.** The reset's To-dos step still lays out three weeks of tiles, for planning ahead, but this week's come first and the rest wait under "Later, in the next three weeks", so its count and the button's agree.
- **Not built.** The reset's recap (the board's own "Still open" list) still counts by the calendar week. On a Sunday the two windows are the same week; midweek they can differ by what's due Sunday to Wednesday next week. It's the board's code, outside this pass.

## (a) This week's plan

What was there: the dinners, a To-dos card listing only to-dos that already had a time (with a check each), a gold line "4 more, due this week, need a time", Kept for you, With Sam, and Something changed? with four tiles. The gold button was Redo the reset.

What was wrong:
1. Nothing could be added or taken off; a row did nothing but check.
2. What still needed a time hid behind a count, and the count was off by one.
3. "Tap anything to change it" wasn't true of a to-do.
4. Checking one off had no Undo and said nothing.
5. The gold button said Redo the reset; the section under it said there's no need to.
6. It opened only once the week was set, and only from the chip.
7. Leaving it to place something meant finding it again.

What V11 does:
- **Built.** The To-dos card is the week's to-dos as a list: Needs a time (gold heading: due this week and not all timed, or timed after it's due), then Has a time (by day and time), with "2 of 7 done" at the top.
- **Built.** Every row: a check (Undo in the line it leaves), the name with where it stands under it ("Due tomorrow · 1 of 3 steps has a time", "Thu, 2:15 pm · Due Saturday"), a gold calendar mark to give it a time, and an x to take it off the list (Undo; it says when its times leave the week with it).
- **Built.** Tap a name: the to-do's own panel (its name, the day it's due, its steps, repeats). Give it a time: the board's place panel on the plan's week, the board showing where it lands. A to-do with some steps timed, or a time after it's due, opens its own panel instead, where each step and each time is.
- **Built.** Whatever the plan opened, closing it brings the plan back a beat later (so a block placed from it is seen landing), with that to-do in view and in focus. Dinner rows do the same.
- **Built.** Add a to-do at the foot of the card: Enter or Add, due the week's last day unless a day is typed ("Call the bank Friday"). A time ("at 3 pm") hands it to Add, which shows where it lands first. The box keeps focus for the next one.
- **Built.** Checked off rows stay where they were, struck through, until the plan closes; nothing jumps.
- **Built.** Open the week is the one gold button. Redo the reset is a quiet line at the end of Something changed?: "Or redo the whole reset".
- **Built.** Something changed? trades Add a to-do (the card does that now) for "Place what's due: drag this week's to-dos onto days", which lays them out on the desk.
- **Built.** Empty and good news: "Everything due this week has a time." or "Nothing due this week, and nothing with a time yet."
- **Built.** Before the reset: the plan opens anyway, its kicker says "Not planned yet", the line says "What's on it so far", and the gold button is the reset that fits the day (Start Sunday Reset, Set up this week, Plan the rest of the week, Finish your reset). Something changed? waits until there's a plan.
- **Built.** The title is on the type scale (30, it was 40; 26 on a phone), and the To-dos column is the widest of the three.
- **Built.** A phone's width: one column, and each row's time and x stack beside the name so the name keeps the width.
- **Not built.** Rename or change the due day in the row itself. Both are one tap away in the to-do's own panel (the name opens it, its Due field is second); a row with its own date menu was cut to keep the rows quiet.

## (b) The To-dos panel

What was there: a long line ("Pick a stack. Its pieces come out on the desk, ready to drag onto a day."), Everything / Lists / Meals and ideas, Find, then six cream cards that all looked the same (Due soon 7 "7 in three weeks", Inbox 1 "1 waiting"...), New to-do, then Dinners and the ideas.

What was wrong:
1. Nothing led: Due soon looked like Bills.
2. Every number was said twice (the badge, then "1 waiting").
3. Two names a card, cut off; nothing could be checked, added, changed, or taken off there.
4. Picking a card put the same to-dos on the desk a second time, smaller.
5. Every list wore the same teal.
6. Dinners and ideas aren't to-dos, and a filter existed only to keep them apart.
7. The stack strip stayed on the screen under the panel.

What V11 does:
- **Built.** One box at the top, "Add or find a to-do": typing filters every list as you go, and Enter adds it to Inbox (with the day, if one is typed). The line under it says what Enter will do. Esc clears it, then closes the panel.
- **Built.** Due this week leads, on a soft gold ground with its heading in gold and its count once: the same to-dos the button counts, as rows that act in place (the same row as the plan's). With nothing due: "Nothing due this week. Next: Clear out the garage, Oct 24."
- **Built.** Then each list as a section in its own color (Inbox gold; Home, Work, and Family take the board's own color for that kind of time; the rest the next color not taken, in list order, so it never changes as to-dos come and go), its count said once, its to-dos as rows. Long lists show five and "4 more". Lists fold, and stay folded on this device.
- **Built.** "Add to Home" at the end of each list adds straight into it; the box stays for the next one, and Esc closes it.
- **Built.** Done, folded at the end, with "Put them away" (Find still finds them, as on the board).
- **Built.** Lay out, on Due this week and on each list, puts its pieces on the desk; the list that's out says "On the desk" in white (white means selected), and a tap puts it away. A line under the box says "Its pieces are on the desk. Drag one onto a day." The panel never draws pieces and the desk never draws check boxes: the panel is where the lists are kept, the desk is where pieces are picked up.
- **Built.** Dinners and the ideas for time together left the panel and its filter; they're at its foot ("Dinners", "Ideas with Sam"), beside "New list".
- **Built.** This week's plan is in the panel's header.
- **Built.** The stack strip steps away the moment the panel opens, and comes back when it closes if a stack is still out. Its first stack is "Due this week".
- **Built.** In Now, with the panel open, the band centers in the room beside the panel instead of running under it.
- **Not built.** Dragging a row from the panel straight onto a day. The board's tray tiles can do it, but the panel's rows would need the board's tile drag opened to the corners; Lay out and the desk's pieces do the same job today.

## (c) The bench and the camera

What was there: a stack laid out beside the week at the size that fits both, with the panel open even smaller; picking a piece up leaned the camera in about 18 percent toward it, which hid more of the week you were carrying it to; nothing changed after the drop. Due soon on the bench held three weeks of to-dos under a count of six.

What V11 does (board/src/scene.js, gated by feel):
- **Built.** The bench's Due this week holds what the count counts; when nothing is due this week it holds the next three weeks and says so ("Nothing this week. The next three weeks."). Its words are V11's ("6 to-dos. Drag a piece onto a day.").
- **Built.** A stack laid out in the week view is framed close: the bench grows where it sits, around its own middle when that fits, and the week stays in view beside it. It zooms only as much as reading needs (a desk unit about 48 px across, so a piece's name is near 14 px), never past 1.7 times: about 1.36 on a 1440 by 900 window, 1.7 with the To-dos panel open, where the desk has the least room. The things on the desk step out while it's framed close, and come back when it's put away.
- **Built.** Picking a piece up eases back out to the week and the bench side by side, on the same critically damped spring as the camera (about half a second, no overshoot), zooming around the piece in your hand so it stays under the pointer (measured: within a few pixels). Where the room is too tight for that, the view gives way as far as it takes to keep the whole week in the room, and the piece in hand is kept on its spot of the screen while the view moves.
- **Built.** No lean in while a piece from the bench is in hand, so the week and the bench stay side by side.
- **Built.** After the drop: the view holds for a beat (0.65 s, so you see it land, the knock and the clack), then eases back in on the bench if it still has pieces to place. A piece that flies home brings it back sooner (0.24 s). With nothing left to place it stays on the whole week.
- **Built.** Not while a side panel is open (the place panel, a to-do's panel): the week shows where that one thing goes.
- **Built.** Calmer motion and the system's reduced motion: no close framing and no travel at all; the week and the bench stay side by side.
- **Not built.** The same close framing in the day view. There the bench stands beside the day at a size that already reads.

## (d) Finding the plan the first time

- **Built.** The menu has This week's plan (Next week's plan on Saturday evening) under Plan, always, set or not yet.
- **Built.** The To-dos panel has it in its header.
- **Built.** The first time the week is set (a reset finishes, a catch up, or the setup), a note rises over the chip a beat after the week clicks into place: "Your plan for the week is here. Dinners, to-dos, and time for you. Tap it any time this week." with See the plan and Got it. Once on this device (`sundayreset.v11.planSeen`), never in a preview or a Dev board, never over a panel, a sheet, the reset, or the coach.
- **Not built.** A way in from the date chip. Its title already means Back to today when you're away from it, and a second meaning would be a surprise.

## Found on the way (board/src/app.js)

- **Built, V11 only.** Dropping a piece or a tray tile called the preview's `settle()` (a later function of the same name took over the drag's own `settle()`), which cleared the bench, the side panel, the picked block, the toasts, and the reset. Dragging a to-do from the reset's To-dos step onto a day ended the reset. V11 calls the drag's by its own name, `settleDrag()`; the other versions are unchanged. The live app (303_86400, src/app.js) has the same two functions.
- **Built, every version.** Placing from the place panel threw "landed is not a function" every time: a flag in `placeIt()` was called `landed` and hid the `landed()` that sets the block down, so the landing's knock and clack never played. The flag is called `arrived` now.

## Decisions for Russell

1. Due this week is the next 7 days (the board's own count), not Sunday to Saturday. The plan lists a to-do due next Monday, with "Due Monday".
2. How close the bench is framed: 1.36 times on a big window, up to 1.7 with the panel open. It's past the 20 percent that's easiest on everyone's eyes, softened by keeping the piece under your hand and never overshooting; one number to tune (`BENCH_ZMAX`, `BENCH_PX` in scene.js).
3. After a drop the camera goes back in on the bench while pieces remain. The other way: stay out on the week until you pick the next piece.
4. The things on the desk (lamp, clock, notepad) step out while a stack is framed close.
5. The To-dos button still lays Due this week out on the desk with the strip; the panel is a tap further (All to-dos). The other way: the button opens the panel and the desk together.
6. Dinners and ideas live at the panel's foot and in the menu, not as cards in To-dos.
7. The plan's title went from 40 to 30 for the type scale.
