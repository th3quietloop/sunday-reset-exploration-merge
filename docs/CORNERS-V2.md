## The list

### The corners
1. Top left: your account, a gold circle with your initial and your first name, in the same espresso as every other chip. In the day view the kind of day sits beside it.
2. Top middle: back, This week (Oct 11 to 17), forward.
3. Top right: Today, Day, Week, Month, Year.
4. Bottom left: hours yours this week (or today, in the day view).
5. Bottom right: Add, To-dos (with what's due as a count), Block library, and the Sunday Reset last, as the call to action. Gold when it's due, quiet the rest of the week.
6. In Today the band sits beside your account on the top, and Today's dock takes the bottom left.

Russell moved these on Oct 13, after the first V2: the account went from the top right to the top left (and from a tan that was hard to read to espresso), hours went to the bottom left, and the Sunday Reset went to the far right.

### Block library, on the desk
7. Block library opens the 3D rack beside the board: in the day view it's the rack beside the day, in the week it's the bench beside the week. Never a flat tray.
8. Pressing it again puts the rack away. The button looks pressed while it's out.
9. From the month or the year, Block library steps in to the week first, since the rack lives beside a week or a day.
10. The flat tray bar never shows in V2, except as the Sunday Reset's steps and Today's dock.

### To-dos, as stacks that hold still
11. To-dos opens the card sheet from Library: Due soon, each list, New to-do, and below them Dinners and time together.
12. The sheet is one fixed size. Filters change what's in it, never its width or height.
13. The cards sit in a fixed grid, five across, all the same size, so a filter with two cards doesn't stretch them.
14. Only the grid scrolls; the title, filters, and Find stay put.
15. Picking a card lays its pieces out on the bench beside the board, in 3D, like Block library. Everything you can place comes from the right side of the desk.
16. The sheet blurs the board behind it, as every pop-up does.

### Settings you can find
17. The account chip shows your initial and your first name, with a chevron.
18. It opens a small menu: you at the top with Settings as the first thing, then Go to (Today, Calendar, To-dos, Block library, Dinners, time together, Focus, the week as a list), then How it works and Sound.
19. Esc or a tap outside closes it, and focus goes back to the chip.

### Color
20. A mark on gold is drawn in espresso, never white.
21. Pressed things are cream with espresso type; everything else is cream on espresso.

### The clock: a Pomodoro Easter egg
22. Point at the clock and it says "Tap for a Pomodoro timer".
23. Tap it: the camera leans in toward the clock, and a small timer card opens beside it.
24. Presets: 25 and 5, 50 and 10, or your own focus and break lengths, in 5 minute steps.
25. It counts down in minutes and seconds (it's a timer you watch, so seconds belong here), with four dots for the rounds and a long break after the fourth.
26. Start, Pause, Skip, Reset. A soft chime at each switch between focus and break.
27. While it runs, the hours chip on the bottom left becomes the timer, with a red dot. Tap it to bring the card back. The tab's title shows the time.
28. It survives a reload.
29. Closing it lets the camera settle back. Esc closes it too.
30. Focus (the session for one thing) stays where it was: F, and the account menu.

## Refined, Oct 13 (after comparing V2 with the 303 board)

31. In the day view the kind of day and the week's seven days sit under the date, as on the board's own date card. A block dropped on a day there moves to it.
32. Saved: a small dot on your circle (green kept, gold saving, red not saved). The menu says it in words and opens Your data.
33. The clock is the first thing kept on the desk after the notepad (`window.SR_clockFirst`), so the Pomodoro is there wherever the desk has room for anything. In the week, month, and year on a laptop window the board takes the whole desk and nothing stands beside it; the Pomodoro is in your menu for those.
34. Time to yourself is good news, never a meter (Russell picked this Oct 13, `window.SR_kindYours`). It reads "to yourself" instead of "yours", and shows only when there is some: the day header (★ 1 h to yourself), month cards, and year cards. A day or a month with none says nothing about it. The corner readout is gone; the bottom left only holds the Pomodoro while it runs.
35. The Sunday Reset says it out loud. Look back: "4.5 h to yourself last week", only when there was some. Your time: "4.5 h to yourself this week, so far", or at zero, "Want some time to yourself this week? There's room for this:" with an open spot ready to add. The recap leaves it out at zero. Never a goal, never a comparison with last week: a goal turns rest into homework, a missed one scolds harder than zero, and it's a streak by another name.
