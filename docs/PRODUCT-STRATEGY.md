# Big things, small blocks (Oct 14, 2026)

Russell's older brother tried Sunday Reset and talked through what he'd want from it. This is what he asked for, what it says about what people are trying to do, and a strategy for the whole app that it fits into. The build follows it (see the end).

## What he asked for, in his words

6. **A plan that adapts when you miss a day.** "If I miss today it would automatically distribute, shuffle stuff around, so that I never got overwhelmed... two, three hours a day, and redistribute my assignments." "You don't fall behind."
7. **Any big goal, not only the bar.** "Nicholas took that finance exam, whatever the thing might be, for it to help you break that down so you can see, this makes it more digestible."
8. **An adaptable schedule overall.** "Having an adaptable schedule."
9. **Built for how ADHD feels.** "Something I've thought about a lot, for time, and my struggles with ADHD."

And from Russell, in the same call: Look ahead already shows what's coming (holidays, buying a gift), and this is the same idea at a bigger scale.

## What people are trying to do

Zoomed out, every one of those asks is one of five jobs.

| Job | When... | I want to... | So I can... |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep up | the house, the laundry, the errands keep piling up | do a little every day instead of one dreaded hour | never face the big version |
| Eat well | dinner is a 5 pm emergency | decide the week's meals once and cook in batches | stop deciding every night and stop wasting food |
| Reach a big goal | there's an exam, a race, a project with a date | get a daily share I can finish | arrive ready without cramming |
| Be ready for what's coming | a birthday, a holiday, a trip is weeks away | start the small prep early enough | never scramble the night before |
| Keep my own time and my people | the week fills up with everything else | protect it before the week fills | have a life, not only a schedule |

What makes all five hard is the same handful of things, and they're sharper with ADHD:

- **Starting.** A big task has no first move. The cure is a first step small enough to start now.
- **Time blindness.** "Clean my room" could be 20 minutes or a weekend. The cure is an estimate, shown as a block with length.
- **Overwhelm.** Seeing all of it at once stops people. The cure is today's share only, with a cap.
- **Falling behind.** One missed day makes the plan feel broken, and people abandon it. The cure is a plan that bends: the miss slides forward and the days ahead stay doable.
- **Decision fatigue.** Deciding what to do and what to eat every day wears people out. The cure is deciding once a week, together, in one sitting.

Sunday Reset already does the last one (the Sunday Reset) and part of the time blindness (time is length on the board). The rest is what this adds.

## The strategy

**Sunday Reset turns everything you're carrying into blocks on real days, at a size you can do, and keeps them there when life happens.**

### The atom is the block
Everything becomes a block you can see, pick up, and place: routines, meals, to-dos and their steps, goal sessions, time with people. One thing to learn, and the drag that makes the app fun works on all of it.

### Four time scales, one desk
- **Now**: the block you're in and the next small thing (Today, the band).
- **This week**: the ritual where the week takes shape (the Sunday Reset).
- **The weeks ahead**: dates with lead time, so prep starts early (Look ahead).
- **The long game**: goals with a date, broken into weekly shares (new: Goals).

### One loop for anything big
1. **Capture** it in plain words (quick add, a to-do, a goal, a date).
2. **Break it down** into steps with a time on each, the first one tiny.
3. **Spread it out** over the days before it's due, a little each day, under a cap.
4. **Do** today's share, and only today's.
5. **Bend, don't break**: a missed piece slides forward on its own, and the days ahead stay under the cap. If it can't fit, the app says so and offers real choices, never a pile.
6. **Look back** on Sunday, then the loop starts again.

### Principles
- Small enough to start: every breakdown's first step is 10 minutes or less.
- Time you can see: every piece has a length on the board.
- A cap on every day: the app never plans more for a day than you said you can give it.
- Plans that bend: misses move forward automatically, with one calm line and Undo. No streaks, no red, no guilt.
- Decide once a week: the reset does the deciding with you; the rest of the week, you do.
- Private by default: breakdowns are made on your device. Anything that would leave it is your choice, asked first.
- Calm, plain words: "Didn't get to it" is a normal button, not a failure.

## How each of his situations plays out

### "Clean my room"
1. Add "Clean my room". Break it down suggests: pick up the floor (10 min), put away laundry (15), make the bed (5), clear the desk (15), dust (10), vacuum (15). About 70 minutes in all.
2. Spread it out: "A little each day, about 15 minutes, done by Saturday." The preview shows one or two steps a day, Monday to Saturday, in open time, after dinner if you like evenings. Place them.
3. Tuesday you skip. Tuesday's step slides to Wednesday, which stays under its 15 minute cap by nudging the rest one day later. Still done by Saturday. One line says so, with Undo.
4. If there aren't enough days left, it says "This needs 2 more days than are left" and offers: do a little more each day, push the due date, or take a step off.

### Meal prep
1. On the meal board, put chicken and rice on Sunday dinner and tap Cook extra: Monday and Tuesday lunch fill with "Leftovers: chicken and rice".
2. Wednesday, tacos, with Thursday lunch as leftovers.
3. The grocery list makes enough for every serving. A Meal prep block can go on Sunday afternoon with what it makes.
4. Or let Suggest meals plan a prep week: two batch cooks and the leftovers they cover, plus fresh nights, from your own meals first.

### The bar exam (or a finance exam)
1. New goal: "Pass the bar exam", on February 24. The app knows the common subjects and suggests them as parts. You can edit them, or add your own for any exam.
2. Your rhythm: weekdays 2 hours, weekends 3, mornings before work. The plan says what that adds up to by the date (about 280 hours), against what you said the goal needs.
3. Each week the reset places that week's sessions in open time, a subject per session, practice tests on a regular cadence, and the last days lighter.
4. Skip Thursday (or tap "Didn't get to it" when it ends): those 2 hours spread over the next days, up to your cap of 3. If the cap can't hold it, the goal says you're 2 hours behind and offers: add Saturday mornings, make days a little longer, or move the date.
5. A trip (Friday to Monday in LA) has no sessions; the plan works around it on its own.
6. The goal shows where you are: hours done, this week's share, on track or how far behind.

### A birthday gift, a holiday, a trip with a baby
Look ahead already turns a date into a "Get ready" to-do with lead time ("Buy a gift for Jill, by October 7"). Now that to-do breaks down (list ideas, pick one, order it, wrap it) and spreads over the days before it. A trip gets a packing breakdown, and goals and spread steps step around the trip's days.

## What we're building

1. **Break it down, made smart.** A much bigger built-in library (housework, errands, admin, school, work, travel, events, health, money, moving, meal prep), a time on every step, a first step of 10 minutes or less, and Smaller steps and Bigger steps to change how fine it is. Made on the device. Steps can be edited before they're added.
2. **Spread it out.** On any to-do with steps: a little each day, on weekdays, on weekends, or days you pick; a cap per day; done by its due date. A preview of the days before anything is placed; one Undo.
3. **Find new times.** A missed step slides forward on its own, under the cap, with a line and Undo. Anything that can't fit says so and offers choices.
4. **Goals.** A goal has a name, a date, parts (subjects, chapters, milestones) with time on each, and a rhythm (which days, how long, when in the day). The plan places this week's sessions; a missed session spreads forward under the cap; it says plainly when the goal is behind and what would fix it. Common exams come with their parts suggested.
5. **A Goals step in the Sunday Reset**, when there's a goal: this week's share of each, placed with one tap.
6. **Today asks once.** The morning after a goal session, one question: did it happen? Yes, Some of it, or No. The plan adapts to the answer.
7. **Cook once, eat twice.** Leftovers linked to the meal they come from, groceries for every serving, a Meal prep block, and Suggest meals that plans a prep week.
8. **Get ready, broken down.** Look ahead's prep to-dos come with steps, spread before the date.

## Intelligence

His third ask is a model that writes the steps. Three ways to get there, in order:

1. **Now, on the device:** the built-in library and rules above. It works offline, costs nothing, and keeps the promise on the site: your week stays on your device.
2. **Next, in the App Store apps:** Apple's on-device model (the Foundation Models framework in iOS 26 and later) can write breakdowns and estimates for anything, still without anything leaving the phone. The native shell passes it to the page through the same breakdown function.
3. **Optional, on the web:** a cloud model (Claude) behind a clear opt-in, for tasks the library doesn't know. This needs Russell's OK: it's a cloud service with a cost per use, and it changes the privacy policy, because a task's words would leave the device.

The breakdown function has one shape for all three, so adding a model is a small change, not a rebuild.
